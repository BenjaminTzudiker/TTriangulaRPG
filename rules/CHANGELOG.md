# Changelog

Every change to the rules notes in `rules/`, newest first. [[Playtest Packet]] is the index.

## How to write an entry

Add the entry under **Unreleased**, in the same commit as the change. One line per change:

`- [[Note]]: what changed, old → new. Why: the reason. Source: where it was decided.`

- **Why** names what the change serves or fixes: a pillar (P1–P4, C1), a review finding, a playtest or sim result. If you don't know why, write *Why: not recorded.* Never guess a reason.
- **Source** says where it was decided, so it can be found again: a review finding (Review F3), a ledger entry (Ledger MV-3), a sim report, or a design chat and its date.
- If the change affects the engine, end with *Engine:* and the component or preset that implements it.

File each entry under one of these headings:

| Heading | For |
|---|---|
| Rules | Anything that changes what a rule means: a new or removed rule, a changed number, cost or procedure. |
| Status | A status marker changing, for example Exploratory → Mechanism open. |
| Clarified | Wording that makes a rule clearer without changing what it means. If you're not sure it's only a clarification, it goes under Rules. |
| Editorial | Structure and formatting: splitting, renaming or moving notes, links, callouts. |

A commit that only fixes typos can skip the entry by putting `[no changelog]` in its commit message.

**Cutting a version:** rename **Unreleased** to the new version and its date, start a fresh empty **Unreleased** above it, and update the version line on [[Playtest Packet]].

## 0.11.4 (2026-11-20)

Shooting in melee provokes when you declare the target, before you pay, and you can then cancel the shot. The engine plays it as the experiment preset `exp-0.11.4`.

### Rules

- [[Ranged Attacks]], [[Opportunity Attacks]], [[Drawing Weapons]]: the opportunity attacks a shot in melee provokes happen after the shot resolves, from enemies threatening you both when you declared it and then → when you declare the target, before you pay anything, from every enemy threatening you then. Then you pay for the shot or cancel it; a cancelled shot costs nothing and isn't an action. You block those attacks with whatever you're holding; drawing a weapon to block them gives up the shot. The procedure is renumbered (paying is now step 2, and the old step 7 is gone), with a new example. Why: the old timing meant checking who threatened the shooter at declaration, carrying that through aim, the roll, the target's dash and blocks, then checking again, and a target that dashed in or out mid-shot made that fiddly. Resolving at once checks reach a single time, matches movement, where an opportunity attack comes when you provoke it, and is more plausible. The designer accepted that point-blank shots get harsher: the enemy next to you always swings first, even the target. A shooter dropped by those attacks loses nothing it had paid. Cancelling and the drawing line stop an archer from drawing a blade to block and then shooting anyway. Source: design chat, 2026-11-20. *Engine:* preset `exp-0.11.4` (`opportunity.point_blank_eligible = "at_declaration"`). The shooter blocks with what it holds, and cancels only when it's incapacitated or can't afford the shot; agents never cancel by choice or draw to block.

### Editorial

- [[Playtest Packet]]: version line 0.11.3 → 0.11.4. Why: cutting 0.11.4. Source: designer's request, 2026-11-20.

## 0.11.1 (2026-11-17)

Holds, and opportunity attacks to go with them. At the end of your turn you can put stamina on enemies as tokens: a creature you hold pays more to leave your reach, and your tokens add to your opportunity attack against it. Opportunity attacks get a rule of their own in place of 0.11.0's one-step placeholder: a small cap for everyone, raised by Finesse and by holds. Hold tokens count toward maximum stamina, and the spear needs two hands for its reach. The engine implements this only as an experiment preset, `exp-0.11.1` (with `exp-0.11.0` for 0.11.0's movement alone); its baseline is still `v0.10.1`.

### Rules

- [[Holds]] (new): at the end of your turn, the tokens you set last turn come back, then you may put any amount of stamina on any enemies as tokens, in your reach or not, then you recover. Tokens stay out until the end of your next turn, whatever happens to the hold, and count toward your maximum stamina. When a creature you hold leaves a square in your reach, it pays 1 + your tokens to avoid your opportunity attack. Your tokens add to your cap for opportunity attacks against it and are spent first, including on the attack its point-blank shot provokes (which shrinks the hold but doesn't end it). They pay for nothing else. A hold ends when the creature leaves a square in your reach (whether it pays, takes the attack, or you decline to attack) or when you lose any HP. Tokens left on a hold that ended, or on a creature that went down, stay out until the end of your next turn. Restated in [[Opportunity Attacks]], [[Stamina]], [[Starting Combat and Turn Structure]], [[Actions and Escalation]], [[Ranged Attacks]], [[Core Concepts]], [[Quick Reference]] (Stamina Costs) and [[Defined Terms]]. Why: finishing an enemy that's losing should take a visible commitment, the way tackle does in EVE, without new chores for the creature in trouble. Small holds press an edge; big ones end a decided fight instead of rounds of doomed retreat, and are a gamble against an enemy that can still hurt you. P1, P2. In a one-on-one chase, a worn-out fighter gets away 30% of the time with holds and 37.5% without (exact, open line). One holder can't keep pinning a creature that can still hit back: a 2-step counterattack gets through 4 stamina of blocks 91% of the time, which ends the hold (exact). Known cost: in a close three-on-one, holds make winning initiative matter more (see [[Not in This Version]]). Source: design chats, 2026-11-15 to 17; `claude/Hold_Rule_Study_2026-11-17.md`, `claude/Hold_Rule_Followup_2026-11-17.md`, `claude/Hold_Rule_Stocktake_2026-11-17.md`. *Engine:* preset `exp-0.11.1`, an experiment.
- [[Opportunity Attacks]] (Making Opportunity Attacks): exactly one step at the weapon's base cost, with no limit but stamina (0.11.0's placeholder) → steps cost the weapon's base +1 per earlier step of the same opportunity attack, starting fresh each time and still not actions; one attack can cost at most 1, +1 per odd Finesse value, plus your hold tokens on the target, which are spent first. So a heavy weapon needs Finesse 3 or a hold to make one. This brings back 0.9's cap (removed in 0.9.1), and [[Finesse]] gets it back as an odd-value bonus. Restated in [[Actions and Escalation]], [[Sample Character]], [[Quick Reference]] (Stamina Costs, Attribute Bonus Table) and [[Defined Terms]]. Why: everyone gets a small opportunity attack, and big weapons have to commit (Finesse or a hold) to punish someone leaving. The designer ruled a cap of 1 raised by Finesse; the step of +1 per odd value is 0.9's. Source: design chat, 2026-11-17. *Engine:* `opportunity.pricing = "fresh"` and `cap_rule = "flat"`, in `exp-0.11.1`.
- [[Stamina]]: your current stamina can't exceed your maximum, and a wound that lowers the maximum lowers it → your current stamina plus your hold tokens can't exceed your maximum. Whenever something would take you over (a wound, your recovery, an ability), you lose the excess and choose where it comes from: current stamina, tokens, or some of each. Restated in [[Holds]]. Why: one rule for every case instead of a special order for each; players are assumed to choose well. Source: design chat, 2026-11-17. *Engine:* after a wound, tokens go first in `exp-0.11.1` (`opportunity.hold_wound_clamp = "tokens_first"`; the better choice once the wound has ended the holds); when recovery would go over, the tokens stay and the extra recovery is lost.
- [[Weapons]]: the spear's hands, **TODO** → one or two, with its reach (1 extra square) only in two hands. Why: so a spear and a shield can't combine reach with the shield's defense, for example to hold a sword fighter 2 squares away. Source: design chat, 2026-11-17. *Engine:* not implemented: the spear builds hold it one-handed with a shield and still reach 2.

### Status

- [[Opportunity Attacks]]: Exploratory (making them) → Mechanism open (avoidance, making them); Numbers placeholder (the cap). Why: the placeholder is replaced by a designed rule. Source: design chat, 2026-11-17.

### Clarified

- [[Opportunity Attacks]] (Avoiding Opportunity Attacks): if an enemy couldn't make even one step of an opportunity attack against you, there's nothing to avoid, so you owe it nothing. Why: with the cap, some enemies (a greatsword at Finesse 1, say) have no opportunity attack at all; the designer agreed nothing is owed. Source: design chat, 2026-11-17.

### Editorial

- [[Holds]]: new note, after [[Opportunity Attacks]] in the reading order. [[Core Concepts]] (Positioning) mentions holding an enemy. Its two open questions (closing in on a holder's reach weapon, and how well tables can size holds) are callouts in the note. Why: the designer asked to keep both in view without an exception for the first. Source: design chat, 2026-11-17.
- [[Open Questions]]: added whether stamina is public (exact numbers, rough bands or nothing) and how it's tracked with more than one pool. Why: it decides how players size attacks and how lethal threats are telegraphed; it came up while testing holds. Source: design chat, 2026-11-17; `design/Fix_Order_and_Dependencies.md`, F6a (Phase 0). The tracking bullet now links [[Holds]].
- [[Not in This Version]]: the extended-contests entry says its holds are control effects, not the hold in [[Holds]]. Source: design chat, 2026-11-17; `claude/Hold_Rule_Study_2026-11-17.md`, section 2.

## 0.11.0 (2026-11-16)

One dash and one escalation. Extra movement on your turn and off-turn movement become a single purchase, the dash, priced on one count of actions that resets when your turn ends. There is a dash phase before every turn, including the first of a fight, with a stated order. Opportunity attacks are a one-step placeholder until they are redesigned. Not in this version: any ranged-side change, the stamina cap, the hold rules and an initiative tiebreaker. The engine implements none of it yet; its baseline is still the `v0.10.1` preset.

### Rules

- [[Actions and Escalation]] (was Actions and the On-Turn Surcharge, and Off-Turn Escalation): two tracks (the on-turn surcharge, reset at the start of your next turn; the off-turn escalation, reset at the start of your turn) → one escalation counting every action since your last turn ended, reset only when your turn ends, as you recover stamina. Actions are a melee attack step, a ranged shot, an aim step and a dash. Restated in [[Melee Attacks]], [[Ranged Attacks]], [[Aim]], [[Weapons]], [[Blocking]], [[Quick Reference]] (Stamina Costs) and [[Defined Terms]]. Why: so that dashing between turns to close a gap limits what you can do on your next turn. With separate tracks, pursuers answered a creature that gave ground for almost nothing: three attackers who act first down a fresh fighter from 7 squares every time, and with this entry and the two below it is 1.4% for 6 stamina (exact, one round; the same 1.4% with two possible targets). P1, P2; Review F10. Known cost: a first strike from beyond free movement is weaker. A fighter who wins initiative 8 / 10 / 12 squares from an archer wins 77% / 54% / 32% of those fights, down from 89% / 79% / 63% (exact two-player solve, open ground). Source: design chats, 2026-11-14 to 16; `claude/v0.11.0_Dash_and_Escalation_Draft_2026-11-16.md`, `claude/Kiting_Check_OnTurn_Options_2026-11-16.md`. *Engine:* not implemented.
- [[Dash]] (was extra movement in On-Turn Movement, and Off-Turn Movement): two purchases (on your turn: 1 stamina flat, 1 square, not an action; off your turn: 1 + the off-turn escalation, 1 square +1 per odd Agility) → one dash: an action, 1 + your escalation, 1 square +1 per odd Agility, usable on your turn, in the dash phase before any turn, or when targeted by a ranged attack. Each time, you can buy any number of dashes, including none. Restated in [[Free Movement]], [[Agility]], [[Sample Character]], [[Quick Reference]] and [[Defined Terms]]. Source: design chat, 2026-11-16. *Engine:* not implemented.

Ties in initiative are left to the initiative rules, which have no tiebreaker yet. Why: Review F14. Source: design chat, 2026-11-16. *Engine:* not implemented (`off_turn.order` has both directions; it needs a restart after each move).
- [[Cover]], [[Ranged Defense]]: moving in reaction to a shot was an off-turn cost → it is a dash, so an action: it raises your escalation until your next turn ends. Why: follows from the one escalation; ruled "for now" on 2026-11-16. Known cost: it weighs most on low Ranged Defense (kiting check, section 4). Source: design chat, 2026-11-16. *Engine:* not implemented.

## 0.10.1 (2026-11-13)

Clarifications from a cold read of the compiled packet, and a new reading order. The reading order now follows what each rule depends on; no rule changed meaning in the move. The three entries under Rules settle points the text left open or stated two ways. The engine implements it as the `v0.10.1` preset, which is `v0.10.0` with the grazing-block entry below, and is the engine's baseline.

### Rules

- [[Blocking]]: a grazing block leaked 1 damage whatever it stopped → the leak is 1 point of the damage that block stopped, so a grazing block with nothing left to stop leaks nothing; perfect blocks count first. A grazing block that stops less than a full defense value still leaks 1, so every worked example is unchanged. Restated in [[Defined Terms]]. Why: the designer's intent is that 1 of the blocked damage leaks, so over-blocking shouldn't add damage. "Perfect blocks count first" is the design chat's reading of blocks being declared together; the designer accepted it for now. Source: cold read of the compiled packet (`claude/Cold_Read_Packet_0.10.0_2026-11-12.md`) and the designer's answers, design chat, 2026-11-13. *Engine:* `blocks.graze_needs_stop = true`, in the `v0.10.1` preset. The block solver chooses the same blocks under both rules, so engine agents play 0.10.1 exactly as 0.10.0.
- [[Opportunity Attacks]]: when you pay to avoid an opportunity attack wasn't stated → you pay as you leave the square, before the enemy says whether it would attack. Why: the designer confirmed the reading. Source: cold read of the compiled packet (`claude/Cold_Read_Packet_0.10.0_2026-11-12.md`) and the designer's answers, design chat, 2026-11-13. *Engine:* already how `engine.procedures.movement.leave` works (the mover answers `AvoidOA` before any enemy is asked); no change.

### Rules

- [[Range Bands]]: short / medium / long ranges, minimum aims and "beyond long misses" → a band width per weapon: no penalty out to one band width, **−2 to the shot's total for each further band** (floored at 0, measured after the target's reaction), and no maximum range. Band width is +1 square per odd Finesse value and −1 square per point of Strength below the weapon's requirement; at 0 or less you can't shoot the weapon. Restated in [[Finesse]], [[Weapons]], [[Quick Reference]] (Attribute Bonus Table) and [[Defined Terms]]. Why: the designer wants range to be plausible with no sudden cutoffs, and prefers linear bands with a flat penalty (with additive aim, geometric bands would let each aim point double the range); −2 per band with a free aim die (see [[Aim]]) makes bows stronger close in, weaker far out, and adds about one point of defense headroom (`claude/Ranged_Bow_Placeholders_2026-11-10.md`, addendum 1). Source: design chat, 2026-11-08/11. *Engine:* `ranged.bands = "linear"` (`band_penalty = 2`) and `ranged.range_measured = "after_reaction"`, in the `v0.10.0` preset; band widths and Strength requirements in the `v0_10` equipment table.
- [[Weapons]]: the bows. Shortbow 3 per shot, 1d6 + FIN, ranges 6 / 12 / 18 → 3 per shot (unchanged), 1d4 + FIN, band width 2, Precise. Longbow 5 per shot, 1d8 + FIN + STR, ranges 8 / 16 / 24 → 4 per shot, 1d8 + STR, band width 3, Strength 1. New warbow: 6 per shot, 1d10 + STR, band width 4, Strength 3, Piercing 1. New superheavy warbow (provisional): 8 per shot, 1d12 + STR, band width 5, Strength 5, Piercing 2. Why: the designer's placeholder bows, with Strength standing in for draw weight. Piercing gives the heavy bows an armor niche without making them deadlier against unarmored targets, and makes longbow vs warbow a real choice (longbow better against soft targets up close, warbow against armor and at range; addendum 3); extra damage dice (2d8, 3d4) pierced armor mostly by adding damage against everyone (addendum 2). The shortbow stays a d4 so it's easy to roll alongside the d6 aim dice, and keeps Finesse in its total (designer's ruling). The superheavy is kept provisionally: it's best against armor at every range, and against soft targets at extreme range (addendum 4). Source: design chat, 2026-11-10/11. *Engine:* the `v0_10` equipment table, used with the `v0.10.0` preset.
- [[Weapons]]: new weapon properties **Piercing N** (ignores N armor; Hammer is restated as Piercing 1, the same effect) and **Precise** (shortbows: Finesse adds to the total but, like aim, not to the wound directly; the aim limit is the higher of the damage die and your Finesse, so unblocked damage up to Finesse reaches HP). Restated in [[Armor]], [[Critical Hits]], [[Attack Procedure]], [[Quick Reference]] and [[Defined Terms]]. Why: the heavy bows need Piercing. Precise is the designer's ruling, with a worked example in [[Weapons]]. A single Precise shot can't drop a typical caster (8 HP), so the shortbow does its work through several shots a turn. Finesse in the total made it much deadlier than the version without it at a shot cost of 2 (`claude/Ranged_Bow_Placeholders_2026-11-10.md`, addendum 7), so the shot cost stays 3: that puts a Finesse 3 shortbow between the Strength 1 and Strength 3 longbows up close and below them at range (addendum 8). The designer is fine with a strong close-range niche against soft targets, paid for by taking big range penalties further out. Source: design chat, 2026-11-11. *Engine:* weapon fields `piercing` (a `hammer` property is Piercing 1) and the `precise` property, in the `v0_10` equipment table.
- [[Actions and Escalation|Actions and the On-Turn Surcharge]]: a ranged shot becomes an action, costing the shot cost + the surcharge, instead of a flat base cost outside the surcharge; the free aim die isn't an action. Restated in [[Ranged Attacks]], [[Aim]], [[Weapons]], [[Quick Reference]] (Stamina Costs) and [[Defined Terms]]. Why: with flat shots, cheap bows fire many shots a turn (the shortbow up to seven, downing a standing caster every time; `claude/Ranged_Bow_Placeholders_2026-11-10.md`, finding 2), and the designer agreed shots should count as actions. ## 0.9

Changes from v0.8. Moved here from the packet's former "What changed from v0.8" section; it predates the headings above.

- **Maximum HP Damage is removed for now** (see [[Not in This Version]]). Everything that gets past blocks and armor is HP damage.
- **The on-turn surcharge** now counts melee attack steps **and aim steps** as actions. Ranged shots pay a flat cost.
- **Opportunity attacks:**
  - Avoiding one costs 1 stamina per enemy, per square you leave.
  - Shooting within an enemy's reach provokes.
  - The stamina you can spend on an opportunity attack is capped (1, +1 per odd Finesse), replacing "+1 stamina for opportunity attacks".
  - Opportunity attacks share an escalation track with off-turn movement.
- **Off-turn movement** can happen after any creature's turn, including your own. It's capped at 4 squares per purchase, and it shares that escalation track.
- **New rules:**
  - Extra on-turn movement.
  - Base movement.
  - Armor's effect on Agility, and Strength requirements for armor.
  - The weapon table.
  - Critical hits.
  - Dying and death (System Shock).
  - Expertise and Skill Point costs.
  - How class trees and tiers are bought.
  - Class core abilities.
  - Losing base defenses at negative Agility.
- **Corrected:**
  - Dodge Defense uses half your Agility.
  - Initiative uses odd Agility and odd Cunning, with no tiebreaker.
  - The Strain example now costs 9, not 7.
  - Casting isn't allowed in armor or with a shield.
  - Characters start at level 1.
- **Removed:** the 10-level tier advancement, and v0.8's editorial scaffolding sections.
