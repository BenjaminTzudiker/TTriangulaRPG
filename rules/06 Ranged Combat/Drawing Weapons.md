# Drawing Weapons

*Status: Exploratory*

This mainly stops archers from shooting and then immediately switching to a melee weapon for free extra defense. A rushed archer can still pull an emergency blade, at the cost of taxing their next turn.
- **Used hands:** once you use a hand to attack or shoot with a weapon, cast a spell, equip or remove a shield, or interact with an object, that hand is **used** by that thing until the start of your next turn (blocking doesn't make a hand used). You can keep using a used hand for that same thing—such as more swings with that weapon, or more spells if used for spellcasting—but you can't freely draw or sheathe with it or change a weapon's grip.
- **On your turn, before acting:** before you attack, shoot or cast on your turn, you can draw or sheathe weapons freely using unused hands. Dropping a weapon or ordinary item is always free.
- **After acting on your turn, or outside your turn:** after you attack, shoot or cast on your turn—or outside your turn (in a dash phase, or in step 4, Reactions, of an attack against you; see [[Attack Procedure]])—drawing follows these rules:
  - **Normal weapon:** an **action** costing the weapon's **base cost + your escalation** (see [[Actions and Escalation]]), requiring the drawing **hand(s)** to be **unused** (one hand for a one-handed weapon, both for a two-handed weapon).
  - **Quick-draw weapon** (daggers, shortswords): if the drawing hand is **unused**, it costs its **base cost (1 stamina)** and isn't an action (doesn't pay or add to escalation). If the drawing hand is **used**, it is an **action** costing **1 + your escalation**.
- **Shields:** shields can't be drawn or dropped like weapons; equipping a shield is an action with a base cost of **5**, and removing one is an action with a base cost of **1**, both on your turn only (see [[Shields]]).
- **Shooting in melee:** if you draw a weapon to block the opportunity attacks your shot provoked, you give up the shot (see [[Ranged Attacks#Procedure|Procedure]]).
