# Drawing Weapons

*Status: Exploratory*

This mainly stops archers from shooting and then immediately switching to a melee weapon for free extra defense. A rushed archer can still pull an emergency blade, at the cost of taxing their next turn.
- **On your turn, before you attack, shoot or cast:** you can draw or sheathe weapons and change a weapon's grip freely. Dropping a weapon or ordinary item is always free.
- **After you attack, shoot or cast, or outside your turn:** once you attack, shoot or cast (on your turn or in a dash phase), you can't freely draw, sheathe or change a weapon's grip until the start of your next turn (blocking and opportunity attacks don't count). Drawing a weapon in these windows—after attacking, shooting or casting on your turn, in a dash phase, or in step 4 (Reactions) of an attack against you (see [[Attack Procedure]])—is an **action** (see [[Actions and Escalation]]):
  - **Normal weapon:** costs the weapon's **base cost + your escalation**, and is allowed only if you haven't attacked, shot or cast since your last turn started (or since the fight began, before your first turn).
  - **Quick-draw weapon** (daggers, shortswords): costs **1 + your escalation**, even if you have already attacked, shot or cast since your last turn started.
- **Shields:** shields can't be drawn or dropped like weapons; equipping a shield is an action with a base cost of **5**, and removing one is an action with a base cost of **1**, both on your turn only (see [[Shields]]). Removing a shield frees that hand immediately—you can switch a weapon to a two-handed grip or cast with it right away. You can't equip a shield on your turn after you've attacked, shot or cast.
- **Shooting in melee:** if you draw a weapon to block the opportunity attacks your shot provoked, you give up the shot (see [[Ranged Attacks#Procedure|Procedure]]).
