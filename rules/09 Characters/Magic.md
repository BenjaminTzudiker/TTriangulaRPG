# Magic

*Status: Locked (access goal); Numbers placeholder (casting requirements); TODO (everything else)*

Magic should be optional for anyone and never required.

**Casting Requirements** (*Numbers placeholder*):
- A free hand.
- No armor (light, medium or heavy), unless an ability says otherwise. (Shields are allowed, subject to having a free hand and meeting the shield's Strength requirement; see [[Shields]].)
- Each spell states its stamina cost and how it works.

**Spells run on the same stamina economy as everything else.** Costs vary from spell to spell. **TODO:** how magic is limited across fights or outside combat now that strain is shelved (see [[Not in This Version]]).

## Direction Being Explored

*Status: Exploratory; not rules yet*

- **Schools as tracks.** A caster tree is divided into spell schools: the eight 5e schools for a wizard, elements such as fire and ice for a sorcerer. Each school is its own track in the tree. You buy its ranks with Ability Points, and extra spells at a rank you've reached with Skill Points.
- **A casting stat per tree.** Each caster tree has one main casting stat, usually mental. A spell's core effect scales with the casting stat of the tree you cast it from.
- **Riders with their own stat.** Some spells also have a secondary effect that scales with a fixed stat that fits it: an enchantment's extra targets with Cunning, an abjuration's temporary protection with Will. A spell shared between trees then suits some trees better than others; a Cunning bard gets the most from a Cunning-rider enchantment without investing in another attribute. The core effect stays worth casting at a low rider stat.
- **Stats that make sense.** Every stat a spell uses should be narratable, so most or all spellcasting will key off mental stats.
- **One-handed vs. two-handed spells.** Some spells may require one free hand and others two, differentiating combat/defensive spells from heavier spells and creating hands-vs.-defense trade-offs alongside shields and melee weapons.
- **Multi-action spellcasting.** Ideally spells usually involve multiple actions (or weak single-action effects) to match melee attack steps and ranged aim, so escalation and action taxes (such as dashing, off-turn quick-draw, or removing a shield) matter to casters.
- **Casting in armor (exploring alternatives to a hard ban).** Instead of banning armor outright, armor may add a stamina penalty to casting on top of its Strength and Agility trade-offs. Candidate shapes to evaluate once spell action structure is settled:
  - `+1 / +2 / +3` stamina (light / medium / heavy armor) per spell tier so more complex spells are hit harder (open caveats: low-level light armor may be too cheap, and class trees don't have native spell tiers beyond tier-unlock abilities);
  - `+1 / +2 / +3` stamina (light / medium / heavy armor) per spell action if spells are multi-action, scaling automatically with spell commitment without needing tiers or multiplication.
