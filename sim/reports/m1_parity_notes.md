## The gate passed

All 143,200 fights behind the review's published fight tables (Tables 2, 3, 4, 7, 9 and the Addendum grid, at the review's trial counts) came out **identical** between the review's simulator and the engine under `review-sim`. The six flagged cells below were rerun at 10× the trials: 84,000 more fights, again with 0 mismatches. Because the engine plays exactly the oracle's fights, no flagged cell can be an engine bug. Each is either sampling variation in the review's original run, or a figure the review computed with code that isn't in `sim/tests/oracle/`.

## Winner untouched and loser HP at level 30

The review's simulator, as kept in `tests/oracle/`, doesn't compute "winner untouched" or "loser ends at". `run_duels` in `experiments.py` reports wins, rounds, first-mover rate and wound sizes only. So Table 3's two columns came from code that isn't in the oracle. At level 1 they reproduce:

- k2: winner untouched 84.9% against the review's 85%;
- chip-kill: winner untouched 64.3% against the review's 63%;
- loser HP: −3.4 to −3.9 against the review's −3 and −4.

At level 30 they don't. Winner untouched is 53.5% ± 0.7 against the review's 59%. The loser ends at −2.44 ± 0.03 against the review's "−3", which would need −2.5 or lower. (The check marks that cell ✓ only because it allows for both sampling and whole-number rounding.)

**For the designer:** F7's evidence ("winner untouched 63–85% of level-1 duels; loser ends at −3 HP mean") is level-1 and stands. The level-30 cells in Table 3 shouldn't be relied on. The engine's values are 53.5% and −2.4 HP under the review's assumptions.

## Not compared

Table 9's and the Addendum's duel columns print one figure per variant without naming the policy, often as a range. The report shows the engine's chip-kill and alpha values beside the printed text, to read by eye. The Addendum's strike columns are exact and all agree (✓). The Addendum rows that use a +2 surcharge don't come from `grid.py` as kept, which never changes `STEP_SLOPE`. They were run with the oracle's `STEP_SLOPE = 2`, in lockstep as well.
